IV. Factual Background

A. Complainant's Prior Rights and Continuous Use

Complainant has operated under the trade name "The Roof Store" in the roof coating and waterproofing industry in South Florida continuously since 1994, and has held and operated the domain theroofstore.net since July 29, 2002 — more than 19 years before the disputed domain was registered.

Complainant's continuous use is corroborated by multiple independent, third-party-verifiable sources not authored or controlled by Complainant:

  • Internet Archive (Wayback Machine) captures of theroofstore.net dating to January 21, 2002, showing continuous operation and a 2007 rebrand transition to "The Roof Store" branding, with continued captures through the present (Exhibit F).
  • Florida Sunbiz records confirming a single, unbroken corporate filing (Document #P02000006036) operating continuously since January 2002 under successive names, under the same officer, Anthony R. Meurer, at every stage (Exhibit H).
  • A prior federal trademark registration, USPTO Reg. No. 3633280 (Serial No. 78903590), filed June 8, 2006 and registered June 2, 2009 on the Principal Register, covering elastomeric roof coatings (IC 002), roofing installation and repair (IC 037), and waterproofing treatment of roof surfaces (IC 040) — the identical goods and services now offered by Respondent. The registration lapsed January 11, 2016 for failure to file a Section 8 maintenance declaration — a procedural/maintenance cancellation, not a finding of abandonment (Exhibit H-2).
  • Contemporaneous media: a television commercial produced circa 1999 and field-installation footage from December 2011, both independently datable via YouTube's platform-recorded upload metadata (Exhibits I, J).
  • A printed marketing brochure referencing theroofstore.net and the company's operation "since 1994" (Exhibit G).

B. Respondent's Registration and Use of the Disputed Domain

Respondent registered theroof.store on August 13, 2021 — 19 years after Complainant's domain registration and 27 years after Complainant's business founding. Respondent operates the domain as an active commercial roofing-supply platform, shipping products nationally, including into Florida, Complainant's primary geographic market (Exhibit A).

Respondent's operator, Dmitry Lipinskiy, had already built a substantial roofing-industry media audience — the "Roofing Insights" YouTube channel and podcast, founded 2017 — before registering the disputed domain in 2021 under a name identical to Complainant's pre-existing mark (Exhibit D). Respondent's own content confirms deliberate adoption of the name as a branding strategy (Exhibits B-1, B-2, D).

C. Documented Commercial Harm Consistent with Confusion

Complainant's Google Search Console data for the query "the roof store" shows an average ranking position of 11.24 over a 16-month window, with 797 impressions producing only 11 clicks (1.38% CTR), against an expected branded-search CTR of 20–40% (Exhibit K, Exhibit E-2). Independent third-party domain analysis (Moz, July 22, 2026) shows Complainant's 24-year-old domain ranking for only 139 keywords against Respondent's 1,300 — a nine-fold gap consistent with search-engine brand-identity confusion (Exhibit P). Complainant's Google Merchant Center account was also removed without stated cause approximately 12–18 months after Respondent's domain launched (Exhibit Q).

V. Grounds for the Complaint (UDRP Policy ¶4(a))

A. The Domain Name Is Identical or Confusingly Similar to a Mark in Which Complainant Has Rights

Complainant has established rights in the mark "THE ROOF STORE" through continuous common law use since 1994 and a prior federal trademark registration (Reg. No. 3633280) covering the identical goods and services, registered more than a decade before the disputed domain existed (Exhibit H-2).

The disputed domain incorporates the entirety of Complainant's mark. The only structural difference between Complainant's domain (theroofstore.net) and the disputed domain is the placement of the word "store" — within the second-level domain for Complainant, and as the top-level domain (".store") for Respondent. Where a TLD forms a semantically meaningful part of a mark, as ".store" does here, the resulting construction can heighten rather than reduce confusing similarity, since the domain reads as a complete rendering of the identical mark. Complainant submits the disputed domain is not merely confusingly similar but functionally identical.

B. Respondent Has No Rights or Legitimate Interests in the Domain Name

Under Policy ¶4(a)(ii) and ¶4(c), once Complainant makes a prima facie showing on this element, the burden shifts to Respondent to demonstrate a legitimate right. No such right is apparent from the public record.

Policy ¶4(c) Safe HarborAnalysis
¶4(c)(i) — Bona fide offering before notice of disputeFails. Respondent registered 19 years after Complainant's domain, 27 years after founding, in the identical industry and overlapping geographic market.
¶4(c)(ii) — Commonly known by the domain nameFails. Respondent's own identity — "Dmitry Lipinskiy" / "Roofing Insights" — predates the domain by four years and was never "The Roof Store."
¶4(c)(iii) — Legitimate noncommercial or fair useFails. Respondent operates an active national commercial platform under the identical mark — not commentary, criticism, or fair use.

Respondent holds no trademark registration of his own for "The Roof Store" or any variant. Complainant held an actual federal registration covering the identical goods and services more than a decade before Respondent's domain existed (Exhibit H-2).

C. The Domain Name Was Registered and Is Being Used in Bad Faith

1. Disruption of a competitor's business (¶4(b)(iii)). Respondent operates in the identical industry, registered a domain identical to Complainant's pre-existing mark nearly two decades later, and ships actively into Complainant's home market.

2. Intentional attraction of users through a likelihood of confusion (¶4(b)(iv)). Complainant's own branded search term now returns page-two rankings with a 1.38% click-through rate against an expected 20–40% (Exhibit K, E-2), and independent domain analysis shows a nine-fold keyword ranking disparity inconsistent with Complainant's seniority advantage (Exhibit P).

3. Privacy/proxy registration combined with other bad-faith indicators. Respondent's use of Domains By Proxy, LLC, combined with the identical mark, same industry, same geographic market, and Respondent's own acknowledgment of deliberate brand adoption, supports a bad-faith finding (Exhibit C, D).

4. Willful blindness / constructive knowledge. Given the 19-year and 27-year priority gaps and an intervening decade-plus of federal registration, Respondent's adoption of an identical name in the identical industry is not plausibly coincidental (Exhibit D).

5. Continuing use following documented notice. Complainant published a dated, publicly indexed notice of prior rights on July 7, 2026, predating this complaint (Exhibit L).

VI. Remedy Requested

Complainant requests that the disputed domain name theroof.store be transferred to Complainant.

Counsel note: UDRP relief is limited to transfer or cancellation; transfer is the stronger outcome and should be the primary request.
