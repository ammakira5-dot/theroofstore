active roof leaks
If you have active roof leaks, do not paint or coat the tiles, as this will trap moisture, accelerate rot, and cause the new paint to peel off rapidly. “The failure point on a tile roof is rarely the tile itself; it is usually the waterproof underlayment beneath it.”, as noted by Quora. Tiles are merely a shedding layer; the true water barrier is the underlayment underneath them. A leak means this underlayment has failed, and painting over the top cannot seal or fix structural water entry. 
You must hire a licensed roofing contractor to perform a targeted underlayment repair before you can safely paint your concrete tiles to extend their life. 
Immediate Emergency Steps
Contain Interior Damage: Place buckets under active drips and move furniture or electronics away from the area. 
Locate the Entry Point: Trace the water inside your attic to find the source. Note that water often travels down rafters, meaning the leak on your ceiling may be several feet away from the actual hole in the roof. 
Document Everything: Take clear photos of the stained drywall, dripping water, and the corresponding area on your roof for insurance documentation. 
Do Not Tarp Directly Over Tiles: Tarping a tile roof requires specialized methods (like sandbags or specialized weights). Nailing a tarp directly into Spanish tiles will shatter them and create significantly worse leaks. 
The Correct Repair Sequence
Lift the Tiles: A professional will carefully remove the Spanish tiles in the leaking area to expose the felt or synthetic underlayment. 
Replace Failed Underlayment: The roofer will cut out the rotted underlayment, inspect the plywood decking underneath for wood rot, replace any damaged wood, and install fresh, high-grade waterproofing underlayment. 
Reset the Tiles: The original tiles are reinstalled over the new barrier. Any cracked tiles are replaced with matching concrete tiles. 
Wait for Total Dryness: Allow the roof to go through several dry, hot days to ensure zero moisture is trapped in the concrete before starting any painting or coating process.
