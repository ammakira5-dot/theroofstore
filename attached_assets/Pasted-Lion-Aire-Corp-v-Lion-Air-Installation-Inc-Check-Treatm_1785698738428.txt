The document is highly relevant as it analyzes specific types of 'actual confusion' evidence (erroneous customer calls, misdirected complaints, and negative online reviews) that the user's case relies upon to prove trademark infringement. The court explicitly validates that a misdirected negative review can constitute actual confusion capable of inflicting commercial injury. Namely, Plaintiff submitted evidence of a negative online review posted to Plaintiff’s Yelp page, which was intended to be posted on LAI’s Yelp page. ... Such negative review could surely inflict commercial injury ... and is sufficient to raise a disputed issue of fact as to whether there was customer confusion regarding Plaintiff and LAI.' Sunenblick v. Harrell
Check Treatment
Relevance: 93.8% · 895 F. Supp. 616, 38 U.S.P.Q. 2d (BNA) 1716, 1995 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 11402, 1995 WL 476175 · 46 citing cases · Filed on: 1995-08-10, District Court, S.D. New York
The document is highly relevant as it provides a detailed judicial analysis of what constitutes probative 'actual confusion' versus non-probative instances like misdirected inquiries or retailer errors. Specifically, the court rejects anecdotal evidence of misdirected calls and store clerk errors as insufficient to prove confusion among 'ordinary prudent purchasers' unless they impact a purchasing decision. The key supporting paragraph states: 'In sum, there is no evidence in the record that during the entire period when the parties were concurrently selling recordings under an “Uptown” designation a customer ever made a purchasing decision under the mistaken impression that MCA or Andre Harrell was the source of Sunenblick’s product. Nor did any customer decline to purchase Sunenblick’s products under the erroneous belief that he was an infringer of MCA’s label.' This matters because it establishes a high bar for proving actual confusion, suggesting that mere misdirected inquiries (like those potentially cited in the user's case) may be insufficient without evidence of actual diverted sales or consumer purchasing decisions.
