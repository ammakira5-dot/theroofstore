Once that extension period expires, the policyholder is required to fully replace the roof to maintain coverage.July 2024: "My Safe Florida Home" Funding Revisions (SB 7028)The Legislation: Governor DeSantis signed Senate Bill 7028, injecting an additional $200 million into the My Safe Florida Home Program.The Update: This update reorganized the grant timeline to prioritize low-income and moderate-income seniors first. The grant provides homeowners $2 for every $1 spent (up to $10,000) specifically to cover wind-mitigation roof upgrades, which help older roofs qualify for insurance discounts.July 2025: Heightened Carrier Discretion on 15+ Year RoofsThe Market Shift: Following the expiration of certain transitional restrictions from earlier special sessions, private insurers gained aggressive leverage.The Update: Under updated underwriting guidelines, carriers across the state began issuing strict non-renewal notices to policyholders with architectural shingle roofs exceeding 15 years of age. Homeowners were forced to either pay out-of-pocket for a complete replacement or scramble to secure a passing 4-Point/Roof Useful Life inspection to stop automatic drop-offs.March 2026: Failure of the Steep-Slope vs. Low-Slope Expansion (HB 815)The Bill: Lawmakers introduced House Bill 815 to try and alter how the 15-year useful life rule applies to different roof architectures.The Outcome: The bill officially died in committee on March 13, 2026. It attempted to force insurers to differentiate rules for low-slope roofs (allowing specialized roof coatings to count toward the 5-year useful life requirement), but it failed to pass into law.June 2026: Citizens Property Insurance Corporate Revisions (SB 1028)The Legislation: Governor DeSantis signed Senate Bill 1028.The Update: Effective June 16, 2026, this bill altered Citizens' structure to help absorb risk from the private market while tightening eligibility. It heavily reinforced that homeowners cannot remain on a state-backed Citizens policy if a private market insurer offers a premium within 20% of Citizens' rate—regardless of whether the private insurer demands a strict roof replacement or inspection.Late 2026 (Upcoming): 9th Edition Florida Building Code OverhaulThe Expected Update: The Florida Building Commission is scheduled to implement the 9th Edition of the Florida Building Code.The Structural Changes: While the 25% partial roof repair flexibility remains intact, the new code will mandate stricter metal roofing attachment standards and broader requirements for secondary water barriers (sealed roof systems) during any partial or full roof replacements.
