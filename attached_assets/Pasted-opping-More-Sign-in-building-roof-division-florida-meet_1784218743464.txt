The Technical Reason It Was Rejected
The proposal to add polyurethane or any sealant to all tile head laps fails or gets voted down primarily because it legally strips the roof of its "Air Permeable" rating. 

ICC Digital Codes
According to Chapter 15 of the Florida Building Code: 
"The components within the prepared roof covering are discontinuously laid and small, with unsealed side and head laps. Air permeable roofing systems shall be applied over sheathed decks... Any roofing system with sealed side or head laps shall not be defined as an air permeable roofing system."

Why Losing "Air Permeability" is a Problem
When a roof division, engineering firm, or code committee votes on this, it is almost always defeated due to the following cascading issues:
Wind Uplift Calculations Change: Air permeability allows wind pressure to equalize above and below the tiles during a hurricane, dramatically reducing wind uplift forces. If you seal the head laps with polyurethane caulk, the system becomes "air-impermeable." This increases the wind uplift load exponentially, meaning the existing fasteners or foam paddies may no longer be legally or structurally sufficient to hold the tiles down under Florida code. 

ICC Digital Codes
Moisture Trapping: Tile roofs are designed as a "water-shedding" system, not a waterproof barrier (the underlayment does the heavy lifting). Sealing head laps creates small dams. Instead of water draining down naturally, any moisture that sneaks past the tiles gets trapped underneath them, accelerating underlayment rot. 

RoofClaim
Voided Product Approvals: Standard concrete and clay tiles are tested and given a Florida Product Approval Number based on unsealed head laps. Altering the system by gluing all the head laps together voids the system’s legal engineering.

Where Polyurethane Is Allowed
Per the FRSA/TRI Tile Installation Manual, polyurethane adhesives and sealants are strictly restricted to cosmetic "weather blocking" (clipping small cut pieces at valleys/hips) or as full structural paddies underneath the tile for basic securement, but never as a continuous caulk line across the exterior head laps.
