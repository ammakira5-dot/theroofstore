## 2. Technical Buying Criteria for Informed Buyers — Full Outline

**Context:** This becomes the core content for bestroofpaint.com's repositioning as "The Informed Buyer's Technical Resource." The goal is to replace the unsupported "1350 psi vs. 300 psi" claim with real, checkable criteria — content that builds trust precisely because it doesn't oversell.

**H1:** How to Evaluate a Roof Coating Contractor — A Technical Buyer's Guide

**Intro framing:**
> Most homeowners can't tell a roofing-grade coating from a repackaged paint product until it fails. This guide covers the specific, checkable criteria that actually determine whether a coating lasts 3 years or 15 — not marketing claims, but the technical specifications you can ask any contractor to show you in writing.

### Criterion 1 — Dry Film Thickness (DFT)
**What it is:** The actual cured thickness of the coating once applied, measured in mils.
**What to check:** Roofing-grade coatings run 20+ mils per system. Decorative roof paint runs 2–5 mils. Ask for the product data sheet (PDS) — it will state DFT directly.
**Why it matters:** Thickness is what creates a seamless waterproof membrane rather than a surface tint. Thin products fail at seams and joints first.

### Criterion 2 — Ponding Water Rating
**What it is:** Whether the coating is rated to remain submerged under standing water without degrading.
**What to check:** Ask specifically — "Is this product rated for ponding water?" Many standard elastomeric coatings are not, and using one on a flat roof that holds water after rain is a common, expensive mistake.
**Why it matters:** Flat roofs are the most common roof type to develop standing water after storms. A non-rated product breaks down exactly where it's needed most.

### Criterion 3 — Written Manufacturer Warranty (Not Just a Verbal Guarantee)
**What it is:** A warranty issued by the product manufacturer, not just a promise from the installing contractor.
**What to check:** Ask to see the actual warranty document before signing anything. Confirm whether it's the manufacturer's name on the warranty or just the contractor's.
**Why it matters:** A manufacturer-backed warranty remains valid even if the installing contractor goes out of business. A contractor-only guarantee does not.

### Criterion 4 — Surface Preparation Requirements
**What it is:** The cleaning, repair, and priming steps required before coating application.
**What to check:** Ask what's included in the quote — pressure or soft washing, tile/crack repair, primer application. A quote that skips straight to "coating application" with no prep step is cutting corners.
**Why it matters:** A coating is only as good as the surface it's bonded to. Skipped prep work is the single most common cause of early coating failure.

### Criterion 5 — Contractor Licensing & Insurance
**What it is:** Verifiable state contractor license and active liability/workers' comp insurance.
**What to check:** Florida license lookup at DBPR.myflorida.com. Any contractor who won't provide a license number should not be hired.
**Why it matters:** Licensing verification takes two minutes and eliminates the majority of bad-actor risk before any money changes hands.
