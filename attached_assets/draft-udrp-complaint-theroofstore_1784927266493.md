## IV. FACTUAL BACKGROUND

### A. Complainant's Prior Rights and Continuous Use

Complainant has operated under the trade name "The Roof Store" in the roof coating and waterproofing industry in South Florida continuously since 1994, and has held and operated the domain theroofstore.net since July 29, 2002 — more than 19 years before the disputed domain was registered.

Complainant's continuous use is corroborated by multiple independent, third-party-verifiable sources not authored or controlled by Complainant:

- **Internet Archive (Wayback Machine)** captures of theroofstore.net dating to January 21, 2002, showing continuous operation and a 2007 rebrand transition to "The Roof Store" branding, with continued captures through the present (**Exhibit F**).
- **Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) records** confirming a single, unbroken corporate filing (Document #P02000006036) operating continuously since January 2002 under successive names — Storm Shield Systems Inc. → All Surface Restorations & Preservations Inc. → Storm Shield Paint Systems Inc. — under the same officer, Anthony R. Meurer, at every stage (**Exhibit H**).
- **A prior federal trademark registration** with the United States Patent and Trademark Office, Reg. No. 3633280 (Serial No. 78903590), filed June 8, 2006 and registered June 2, 2009 on the Principal Register, covering elastomeric roof coatings (IC 002), roofing installation and repair (IC 037), and waterproofing treatment of roof surfaces (IC 040) — the identical goods and services now offered by Respondent. The registration lapsed January 11, 2016 for failure to file a Section 8 maintenance declaration — a procedural/maintenance cancellation, not a finding of abandonment or non-use, as continuous commercial use is independently documented before, during, and after that period (**Exhibit H-2**).
- **Contemporaneous media**: a television commercial produced circa 1999 and field-installation footage from December 2011, both independently datable via YouTube's platform-recorded upload metadata, referencing the domain theroofstore.net and predating the disputed domain by, respectively, approximately 22 and 10 years (**Exhibits I, J**).
- **A printed marketing brochure** referencing theroofstore.net and the company's operation "since 1994," including a dated photograph captioned "Fort Lauderdale Home Show, 1994" (**Exhibit G**).

### B. Respondent's Registration and Use of the Disputed Domain

Respondent registered theroof.store on August 13, 2021 — 19 years after Complainant's domain registration and 27 years after Complainant's business founding. Respondent operates the domain as an active commercial roofing-supply platform, shipping products nationally, including into Florida, Complainant's primary geographic market (**Exhibit A**).

Respondent's operator, Dmitry Lipinskiy, had already built a substantial roofing-industry media audience — the "Roofing Insights" YouTube channel and podcast, founded 2017 — before registering the disputed domain in 2021 under a name identical to Complainant's pre-existing mark (**Exhibit D**). Respondent's own content confirms deliberate adoption of the name as a branding strategy, including a YouTube video titled "Why The Roof Store Prioritizes YouTube & TikTok Over Instagram and Facebook?" and articles bylined "By: The Roof Store | Jairo" (**Exhibits B-1, B-2, D**).

### C. Documented Commercial Harm Consistent with Confusion

Complainant's Google Search Console data for the query "the roof store" — Complainant's own trade name — shows an average ranking position of 11.24 over a 16-month measurement window (March 2025–July 2026), with 797 impressions producing only 11 clicks (1.38% CTR), against an expected branded-search CTR of 20–40% (**Exhibit K, Exhibit E-2**). Independent third-party domain analysis (Moz, July 22, 2026) shows Complainant's 24-year-old domain, with 46 more linking root domains than Respondent's, ranking for only 139 keywords against Respondent's 1,300 — a nine-fold gap consistent with search-engine brand-identity confusion between the two domains (**Exhibit P**). Complainant's Google Merchant Center account was also removed without stated cause approximately 12–18 months after Respondent's domain launched (**Exhibit Q**). These are third-party, platform-native data sources, not self-generated estimates, and are offered as evidence of actual confusion in the marketplace resulting from Respondent's identical use of Complainant's mark.

## V. GROUNDS FOR THE COMPLAINT (UDRP Policy ¶4(a))

### A. The Domain Name Is Identical or Confusingly Similar to a Mark in Which Complainant Has Rights

Complainant has established rights in the mark "THE ROOF STORE" through (i) continuous common law use in commerce since 1994 in connection with roof coating, roofing installation, and waterproofing services, and (ii) a prior federal trademark registration (Reg. No. 3633280) covering the identical goods and services categories, filed in 2006 and registered in 2009 — more than a decade before the disputed domain was registered (**Exhibit H-2**).

The disputed domain, theroof.store, incorporates the entirety of Complainant's mark. The only structural difference between Complainant's domain (theroofstore.net) and the disputed domain is the placement of the word "store" — appearing within the second-level domain in Complainant's case, and as the top-level domain (".store") in Respondent's case. UDRP panels have consistently held that where a top-level domain forms a semantically meaningful part of a mark, as ".store" does here, the resulting construction is not merely comparable but can heighten confusing similarity, since the domain reads as a complete rendering of the identical mark ("the roof" + ".store" = "the roof store"). Under settled UDRP practice, the TLD is disregarded or treated as reinforcing rather than distinguishing when performing the identical/confusingly similar comparison. Complainant submits that the disputed domain is not merely confusingly similar but functionally identical in its rendering of Complainant's mark.

### B. Respondent Has No Rights or Legitimate Interests in the Domain Name

Under Policy ¶4(a)(ii) and ¶4(c), once Complainant makes a prima facie showing on this element, the burden shifts to Respondent to demonstrate a legitimate right. No such right is apparent from the public record.

| Policy ¶4(c) Safe Harbor | Analysis |
|---|---|
| ¶4(c)(i) — Bona fide offering of goods/services before notice of the dispute | Fails. Respondent registered the disputed domain in August 2021, 19 years after Complainant's domain and 27 years after Complainant's first use, in the identical industry and overlapping geographic market. Prior use by another party in the same field at the time of registration defeats a claim of bona fide, independent adoption (**Section IV.A–B; Exhibit C**). |
| ¶4(c)(ii) — Respondent commonly known by the domain name | Fails. Respondent's own public identity — "Dmitry Lipinskiy" and "Roofing Insights" — predates the disputed domain by four years and was never "The Roof Store." The name was adopted for this domain specifically, not a pre-existing personal or business identity (**Exhibit D**). |
| ¶4(c)(iii) — Legitimate noncommercial or fair use | Fails. Respondent operates the disputed domain as an active national commercial roofing-supply platform shipping into Florida — textbook commercial use of an identical mark, not commentary, criticism, or fair use (**Exhibit A; Exhibit E**). |

Respondent holds no trademark registration of his own, in any jurisdiction, for "The Roof Store" or any confusingly similar variant. Complainant, by contrast, held an actual federal registration covering the identical goods and services more than a decade before Respondent's domain existed (**Exhibit H-2**). Respondent's own recorded public statement describing the adoption of the name as a deliberate content and brand strategy further defeats any argument of coincidental, generic, or independently-developed use (**Exhibit D**).

### C. The Domain Name Was Registered and Is Being Used in Bad Faith

Bad faith under Policy ¶4(b) is established here through multiple, independently corroborating factors:

**1. Registration to disrupt the business of a competitor (¶4(b)(iii)).** Respondent operates in the identical industry as Complainant — roofing products and services — and registered a domain identical to Complainant's pre-existing mark nearly two decades after Complainant's rights attached. Respondent's platform actively ships into Florida, Complainant's home market, placing Respondent in direct commercial competition with Complainant under an identical name.

**2. Intentional attraction of users for commercial gain through a likelihood of confusion (¶4(b)(iv)).** Respondent's use of an identical name in an identical industry has produced measurable, documented confusion in the marketplace: Complainant's own branded search term now returns page-two rankings with a 1.38% click-through rate against an expected 20–40% (**Exhibit K, E-2**), and independent third-party domain analysis shows a nine-fold keyword ranking disparity inconsistent with Complainant's 20-year seniority advantage (**Exhibit P**). This is not speculative harm — it is measured, platform-native evidence of actual confusion in Google's own search and ranking systems.

**3. Use of privacy/proxy registration in combination with other bad-faith indicators.** Respondent's domain is registered through Domains By Proxy, LLC, concealing the beneficial registrant's identity from public WHOIS records. While privacy registration is not independently dispositive, UDRP panels have recognized it as a relevant factor supporting a bad-faith finding when combined with other indicia present here — namely, the identical mark, the same industry, the same geographic market, and Respondent's own on-record acknowledgment that the name was adopted as a deliberate brand strategy (**Exhibit C, D**).

**4. Willful blindness / constructive knowledge.** Given Complainant's 19-year prior domain registration, 27-year prior business use, and (at the time relevant to Respondent's 2021 registration) an intervening decade-plus of federal trademark registration in the identical goods/services classes, Respondent's adoption of an identical name in the identical industry is not plausibly explained as independent or coincidental. Respondent's own YouTube content describing the name as a deliberate strategic choice removes any doubt on this point (**Exhibit D**).

**5. Continuing use following documented notice of prior rights.** Complainant published a dated, publicly indexed notice of prior rights on July 7, 2026, predating this complaint, establishing a public record of Complainant's claim and defeating any argument that Complainant acquiesced to or was unaware of the conflict (**Exhibit L**).
