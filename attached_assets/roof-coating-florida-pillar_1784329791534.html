What Is Roof Coating?

Direct Answer

Roof coating is a liquid-applied elastomeric, polyurethane, or silicone membrane that is sprayed or rolled directly onto an existing roof surface. It cures into a seamless, fully-adhered waterproof layer that protects against water infiltration, UV degradation, mold, and — in certified systems — wind uplift. Unlike roof paint, professional roof coating is formulated to roofing-grade specifications and is designed to last 10–25 years.

Professional roof coating systems are manufactured to standards that are fundamentally different from decorative roof paint. A roofing-grade coating cures into a thick, flexible rubber membrane — typically 20–40 mils of dry film thickness — that bonds directly to the roof substrate. Because there are no seams or joints in a liquid-applied system, it eliminates the primary failure point of conventional tile and flat roofing: the gaps between materials where water enters.

Why Florida Roofs Need Coating More Than Any Other State

Direct Answer

Florida roofs degrade faster than roofs anywhere else in the continental United States due to a combination of intense UV exposure, extreme humidity, year-round mold and algae growth, and seasonal hurricane-force winds. A 20-year-old Florida tile roof has often experienced more structural stress than a 40-year-old roof in a temperate climate — making protective coating one of the most cost-effective maintenance investments a Florida property owner can make.

The Four Florida Roof Killers

1. UV and Solar Heat Loading. Florida receives more direct solar radiation than any other contiguous U.S. state. Prolonged UV exposure oxidizes and embrittles roofing membranes, fades tile glazing, and accelerates the breakdown of sealants around penetrations. The Smart Shield (RP2) system's BASF Cool Pigment Technology reflects solar energy at a Thermal Solar Reflectance (TSR) index up to 32% higher than standard coatings — measurably reducing attic temperatures and cooling costs by 15–25% per Florida Power & Light (FP&L) assessments.

2. Humidity and Moisture Cycling. South Florida averages over 75% relative humidity year-round. Daily moisture cycling — condensation in the morning, solar heating in the afternoon — expands and contracts roofing materials repeatedly. Without a seamless waterproof membrane, this cycling drives water into grout lines, tile underlap joints, and hairline cracks. The Roof Store's liquid rubber systems cure into a monolithic surface that moves with the substrate rather than cracking under thermal stress.

3. Mold and Algae (Gloeocapsa magma). The dark black streaking visible on Florida tile roofs is caused by the cyanobacterium Gloeocapsa magma, which thrives in Florida's warm, humid conditions. Left untreated, biological growth accelerates tile degradation and triggers insurance inspection failures. The Fungal Shield (RP1) system is specifically formulated to eliminate and prevent biological growth — and is the first treatment step before any coating application.

4. Hurricane Wind Uplift. Florida building codes require roofing systems to resist wind uplift forces generated by Category 1–5 hurricanes. Standard tile roofs, while heavy, can lose individual tiles at sustained winds above 90 MPH. The Roof Shield (RP3) monolithic system bonds all tiles together into a single continuous surface, achieving 5× the wind uplift resistance of a standard new tile roof — validated by Dade County TAS-106 testing at 135+ MPH.

Types of Roof Coating in Florida

Three primary coating chemistries are used on Florida roofs. The right choice depends on roof type, condition, intended goal, and budget. The Roof Store determines the appropriate formulation grade after a manufacturer's inspection — not before.

Grade 1 — Elastomeric (Acrylic Rubber)

Elastomeric coatings are water-based acrylic systems that cure into a flexible rubber membrane. They are the most widely used roof coating chemistry in Florida, well-suited to tile roofs in good condition that need waterproofing, UV protection, and color restoration. Elastomeric systems are the formulation base for The Roof Store's Fungal Shield (RP1) and Smart Shield (RP2) systems. Standard warranties typically range from 10–15 years.

Grade 2 — Polyurethane

Polyurethane formulations offer higher tensile strength and impact resistance than standard elastomeric coatings, making them appropriate for roofs with moderate wear, heavier foot traffic surfaces, or greater structural movement requirements. The Roof Store specifies polyurethane grades on roofs where enhanced durability is indicated after inspection. Enhanced warranties are available for polyurethane-grade applications.

Grade 3 — Silicone

Silicone roof coatings are the highest-performance formulation available for Florida conditions. Unlike elastomeric and polyurethane coatings, 100% silicone systems are fully rated for standing water (ponding) — a critical requirement for flat commercial roofs where water pooling is common after Florida's heavy rain events. Silicone formulations do not absorb water, do not re-emulsify after saturation, and maintain their properties for decades. The Roof Store's silicone-grade systems carry product warranties of up to 50 years and are the standard specification for commercial flat roof applications.

How Roof Coating Affects Your Florida Homeowners Insurance

Direct Answer

Professional roof coating applied by a licensed Florida contractor does not void homeowners insurance. Under Florida Senate Bill 2-D (2022) and a Florida Governor's Bulletin issued in March 2023, homeowners have the legal right to an Additional Roof Life Certification — a licensed contractor or engineer's affidavit that certifies the roof's remaining useful life after coating. This certification legally protects the homeowner's insurance coverage and supersedes any insurance agent opinion to the contrary.

The Insurance Crisis and What It Means for Roof Coating

Florida's homeowners insurance market has been in crisis since 2021. Insurers — particularly Citizens Property Insurance — began canceling policies on homes with roofs over 15–20 years old, citing useful life concerns. This created a false narrative: that a roof must be replaced to retain insurance. The legal reality is more homeowner-friendly.

Florida Law: What You Actually Have the Right to Do

Florida Senate Bill 2-D (2022) prohibits insurance companies from refusing to issue or renew a policy solely because a roof has less than 25% of its useful life remaining, provided that useful life is certified by a licensed Florida roofing contractor or engineer — not estimated by an insurer.

Florida Governor's Bulletin (March 2023) reaffirmed homeowners' legal right to an Additional Roof Life Certification (ARLC). An ARLC is a signed affidavit from a licensed contractor or engineer stating the coated roof's remaining certified useful life. Insurance companies are legally required to accept a valid ARLC as documentation of roof condition.

The Roof Store can issue Additional Roof Life Certifications for roofs that have received a qualifying coating system. A properly coated Florida tile roof can be certified for an additional 15–25 years of useful life — keeping your insurance policy active without replacing the roof.

⚠️ Warning: The Misinformation to Watch Out For

Some insurance agents tell homeowners "if you seal or paint your roof, you'll lose your coverage." This is false — and contradicted by Florida statute and the Governor's official bulletin. If you have been told this, request the legal basis in writing. In most cases, the agent is either misinformed or using the statement to push a full roof replacement that is not legally required. A licensed roofing contractor's ARLC affidavit carries legal authority that an agent's verbal opinion does not.

What TAS-106 Means

TAS-106 (Test Application Standard 106) is the Florida Building Code's standard for measuring the wind uplift resistance of roof assemblies. Products must be physically tested on actual roof substrates under controlled conditions. The uplift force required to detach the roofing assembly from the structure is measured and compared against code-required resistance levels for hurricane wind zones.

The Roof Store's Roof Shield system achieves this by filling every gap, void, and overlapping joint between tiles with multiple product layers — binding the entire tile surface into a monolithic structure. A standard Spanish tile roof held down only by its own weight can lose individual tiles at sustained winds of 80–100 MPH. The Roof Shield monolithic system resists uplift at more than five times that force — making it structurally equivalent to a poured roof deck rather than a tiled surface.

Why No Other Coating Has TAS-106 Certification

TAS-106 testing requires a physical test assembly on an actual roof structure. The test costs tens of thousands of dollars to conduct and requires the product to be applied by the manufacturer's own licensed applicators under controlled conditions. Generic elastomeric paints and coatings are not designed to bond tiles into a monolithic surface, and therefore cannot pass the uplift test. The Roof Store invested in this certification because the company is both the product manufacturer and the contractor — giving us full control over formulation, application protocol, and test compliance.

How to Choose a Roof Coating Contractor in Florida

Direct Answer

A qualified Florida roof coating contractor must be licensed as a Florida Roofing Contractor (license type CCC or RC, verifiable at the Florida DBPR online system), carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance, use products manufactured to roofing-grade standards, provide a written manufacturer warranty, and be knowledgeable about Florida's insurance inspection requirements and the Additional Roof Life Certification process.

Six Questions to Ask Any Florida Roof Coating Contractor

  1. What is your Florida roofing contractor license number, and can I verify it? — A licensed Florida roofing contractor can be verified instantly at the Florida DBPR website. Any contractor who refuses to provide their license number should not be hired.
  2. Are your coating products manufactured to roofing-grade specifications? — Ask for the product data sheet (PDS) and technical safety data sheet (SDS). If the product is paint-grade rather than roofing-grade, the warranty and performance claims will not hold up.
  3. Do you carry the product warranty, or does the manufacturer? — A manufacturer-backed warranty (issued by the product manufacturer, not just the contractor) remains valid regardless of whether the contractor remains in business.
  4. Is your product TAS-106 certified? — Only The Roof Store's Roof Shield (RP3) holds this certification. If a competitor claims TAS-106 compliance for a liquid coating system, request the actual test report number from the Miami-Dade BCCO.
  5. Can you issue an Additional Roof Life Certification for insurance purposes? — This requires that the contractor be licensed and that the product meet the quality threshold an insurance inspector will accept.
  6. What does your inspection and preparation process include? — A professional system requires cleaning (pressure or soft wash), fungal treatment, repairs to damaged tiles, primer application, and then coating application in specified layers. Any contractor quoting without a site inspection or skipping preparation steps is cutting corners that will shorten the system's lifespan.
