3. Technical Health

3.1 Crawlability & Indexability

✅ robots.txt — Clean

Correctly allows all crawlers. Blocks only /admin, /trademark-evidence-file, /trademark-monitoring-log. Sitemap declaration present.

⚠️ Sitemap URL Mismatch — www vs non-www

robots.txt declares the sitemap at https://www.theroofstore.net/sitemap.xml (www), but the canonical domain resolves as https://www.theroofstore.net/. The site appears to redirect consistently to www. Confirm that Google Search Console is verified for both www and non-www variants, and that the preferred domain is set.

Fix: Submit both property variants in GSC. Set www as preferred. Ensure sitemap is submitted under the www property.

✅ Sitemap Coverage — Comprehensive (100+ URLs)

Sitemap includes homepage, all product pages, all blog posts, all service areas (county + city level) across Broward, Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, and Monroe counties. Last-modified dates all set to 2026-07-17 — correct and recent. Priority values appropriately tiered (homepage 1.0, city pages 0.5).

⚠️ Duplicate Meta Tags in Source HTML

The HTML source contains every meta tag (og:title, og:description, robots, twitter:card, geo.region, ICBM, etc.) duplicated exactly twice. This is a template render issue — the page appears to be injecting the <head> block twice, or meta tags are being output by both a layout component and an individual page component.

Fix: Inspect the <head> rendering in your site framework. Deduplicate meta tag injection. This affects every page on the site and may confuse crawlers, although Google typically uses the first instance only.

✅ Canonical Tags — Correctly Implemented

All audited pages declare self-referencing canonical URLs pointing to the www variant (e.g., https://www.theroofstore.net/products/roofshield). No cross-page canonical conflicts detected.

✅ Robots Directives — index, follow across all pages

All audited pages have index, follow set. No accidental noindex detected on any public page.

Comparing the Fort Lauderdale and Broward County city pages, the content follows an identical template structure: same H3 headings ("Our Services in [City]", "Why [City] Homeowners Choose Us", "Our Roof Coating Systems", "Comparing Roof Paint vs. Roof Coating in [City]"), with only the city name and a short 2–3 sentence "About [City]" paragraph varying between pages. This pattern across 100+ cities creates a near-duplicate content cluster that Google may consolidate into the county parent page rather than ranking each city page individually.

Fix Strategy — Differentiate at least the top 20 city pages:

  • Add at least one city-specific before/after project reference or photo.

✅ RoofingContractor Schema — Well-Structured on All Service Pages

The RoofingContractor markup includes: name, alternateName, legalName, telephone, email, postal address, GeoCoordinates, priceRange, description, areaServed, hasOfferCatalog, sameAs, aggregateRating, and openingHoursSpecification. This is a comprehensive implementation that directly supports Local Pack eligibility.

✅ FAQPage Schema — Implemented on 4 Pages

FAQPage schema present on Homepage, FAQ page, Roof Shield PDP, and Blog index. Questions and acceptedAnswers are fully populated. This is eligible for FAQ rich results in Google SERPs.

⚠️ Product Schema: aggregateRating Has Only 9 Reviews

The Product schema on Roof Shield and the RoofingContractor schema on the homepage both declare aggregateRating: { ratingValue: "5.0", reviewCount: "9" }. Google's threshold for displaying star ratings in Rich Results is typically 3+ reviews, but a reviewCount of 9 is very low and may reduce CTR impact vs. competitors with 50–200+ reviews. The rating source appears to be the in-house schema declaration rather than a verified third-party platform.

Fix: Integrate Google Reviews or a verified review platform (e.g., Birdeye, NiceJob). Update the aggregateRating in schema to pull from a live, verifiable source. Actively collect reviews from past customers to reach 50+ within 90 days.

⚠️ Blog Posts Missing BlogPosting Schema on Individual Post Pages

The Blog index page includes Blog + BlogPosting schema for all 4 posts in aggregate. However, individual blog post pages (e.g., /blog/roof-coating-can-save-your-home-and-insurance-policy-in-florida/) were not audited individually. These pages should each have their own BlogPosting schema with author, datePublished, dateModified, headline, and articleBody. Without it, blog posts miss eligibility for Article Rich Results.

Fix: Ensure each blog post page includes {"@type":"BlogPosting"} with author (Organization or Person), datePublished, headline, and image.

⚠️ Pricing Page Schema Too Minimal

The pricing page only has a PriceSpecification wrapper without any Service or Offer schema linking the prices to specific services. This misses the opportunity for richer structured data that could support Google's display of pricing information.

Fix: Add Service schema with hasOfferCatalog → Offer → priceSpecification for each of the three systems. This enables richer interpretation by both Google and AI search engines.

✅ BreadcrumbList — Correctly Implemented Site-Wide

All audited pages include accurate BreadcrumbList schema with correct position, name, and item URL values reflecting the full URL hierarchy (Home → Service Areas → Broward County → Fort Lauderdale, etc.).
