Direct Answer

You can't act against theroof.store's site, and you shouldn't try — negative SEO or retaliatory tactics backfire and weaken any legal position. Every recommendation below strengthens theroofstore.net's own entity signals so search engines have no reasonable basis to keep splitting authority between the two domains.

1 Fix the Duplicate Meta Tag Bug — Close This First

Every page on theroofstore.net currently renders all meta tags twice — title, description, Organization schema references, everything. This has been open the longest of any technical finding across every audit. Every other fix on this list is undermined while it stays open, because it means every page sends conflicting entity signals simultaneously. Fix this before anything else.

2 Add disambiguatingDescription to Organization Schema

This is the one schema.org property built specifically for this exact situation — two entities with similar names that search engines need to tell apart. Add it inside the existing Organization schema block on the homepage and every major page:

"disambiguatingDescription": "The Roof Store (Storm Shield Paint Systems Inc.), 
founded 1994 in Davie, Florida. Not affiliated with theroof.store 
or any similarly-named domain registered after 2021."

This isn't a legal statement or an attack — it's a factual, machine-readable disambiguation signal that directly helps search engines and AI answer engines understand there are two distinct entities. It's a legitimate, documented schema property, not a gray-area tactic.

3 Carry the Same Entity Signal on Every Page

The strongest Organization schema (full address, phone, foundingDate, sameAs, aggregateRating) currently lives mainly on the homepage. Every page — city pages, product pages, blog posts — should carry the same core identity block: same legalName, same foundingDate, same sameAs array. Signal density matters — the more pages that consistently repeat the same entity facts, the more confidently search engines resolve which "The Roof Store" is the 1994 original.

4 Expand and Verify the sameAs Array

  • BBB profile URL
  • Verified Google Business Profile URL (once locked-out profiles are recovered — see below)
  • The historical YouTube channel referenced in the trademark evidence file — a real, independently-dated asset tying back to the 1994/2002-era brand history
  • Any verified Facebook/LinkedIn business pages

Each verified link is another anchor point search engines can use to confirm which entity is real, established, and continuously operated.

5 Recover the Locked-Out Google Business Profiles

Three GMB profiles are currently inaccessible, including one registered under the exact legal entity name. A profile that can't be updated or verified can't reinforce anything — it just sits as noise. Recover these through Google's official "Request Access" flow.

6 Claim the Google Knowledge Panel, If One Exists

Search the business name while logged into the account tied to the primary Google Business Profile. If a Knowledge Panel appears, Google provides a "Claim this knowledge panel" flow directly in search results — the correct, sanctioned channel for flagging conflated entity data.

7 Pursue Google Merchant Center Reinstatement

This is a real, recoverable asset — not a disambiguation project, a restoration of something already held that was removed without stated cause. Filing doesn't depend on resolving the broader dispute first.

8 Fix NAP Consistency Across Every Controlled Citation

Every directory listing, GMB profile, and partner/distributor site referencing theroofstore.net needs identical name, address, and phone. At least one confirmed mismatch exists today. Inconsistent NAP weakens local entity confidence — fixing it removes one more thing for search engines to have to resolve.

9 Grow Verified Reviews Past the Credibility Threshold

Reviews tied to a verified, NAP-consistent GMB profile are one of the strongest "this is the real, established business" signals available. Each new verified review is also a dated, third-party data point reinforcing continuous operation.

10 Keep Building Citable, Dated, Unique Content

Original, well-sourced, frequently updated content earns algorithmic trust over time — and trust compounds in favor of the party with the longer documented history. This is a genuine structural advantage no schema change can substitute for.

⚠️ What Not to Do

  • Don't touch the competing site, its links, or its reviews in any way — no reporting campaigns, no coordinated actions, nothing retaliatory. Anything resembling an attack undermines any legal position and can become evidence used against you.
  • Don't attempt reciprocal or manipulative link tactics in either direction — both are policy violations and both are detectable.
  • Don't write disambiguation language that reads as an attack — keep it factual, dated, and unemotional, like the schema example above.

Sequencing

  1. Fix the duplicate meta tag bug — no dependencies, start now.
  2. Add disambiguatingDescription and expand sameAs — same content pass.
  3. Start the GMB recovery requests — Google's verification timeline runs independently in the background.
  4. File the Merchant Center reinstatement — already drafted, just needs submission.
  5. Fix the Port St. Lucie NAP mismatch — quick, closes an open inconsistency.
  6. Continue review growth and content publishing — the long game, already moving in the right direction.
