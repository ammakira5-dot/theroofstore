## 6. INSURANCE & LEGAL REFERENCES

### Florida Senate Bill 2-D (2022)
- **What it does:** Prohibits insurance companies from refusing to issue or renew a homeowners policy solely because a roof has less than 25% of its useful life remaining — provided that remaining life is certified by a licensed Florida roofing contractor or engineer.
- **Key restriction:** The useful life assessment must come from a licensed inspector, not the insurer.
- **Citation format:** Florida Senate Bill 2-D (2022) / SB 2-D

### Florida Governor's Bulletin (March 2023)
- **What it does:** Reaffirmed Florida homeowners' legal right to an Additional Roof Life Certification (ARLC).
- **Legal effect:** An ARLC affidavit legally supersedes insurance agent opinions about roof condition.
- **Citation format:** Florida Governor's Bulletin, March 2023

### Additional Roof Life Certification (ARLC)
- **What it is:** A signed affidavit from a licensed Florida roofing contractor or engineer certifying the coated roof's remaining useful life.
- **Insurance effect:** Insurance companies are legally required to accept a valid ARLC as documentation of roof condition.
- **Extended life:** A properly coated Florida tile roof can be certified for an additional 15–25 years of useful life.
- **Who can issue:** Licensed Florida Roofing Contractors — including The Roof Store.

### The Misinformation to Debunk
> "If you seal or paint your roof, you'll lose your insurance."

**Correct answer:** False. This is contradicted by Florida statute (SB 2-D) and the Governor's official bulletin. Insurance agents who make this claim are either misinformed or pushing unnecessary roof replacement. A licensed contractor's ARLC affidavit carries legal authority that an agent's verbal opinion does not.

## 12. GEO CONTENT RULES — APPLY TO EVERY PAGE

These rules make content extractable and citable by Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT, and other AI search systems.

### Rule 1 — Direct Answer First
Open every H2 section with a 1–2 sentence direct answer to the implied question in that heading. No preamble. No setup paragraph before the answer.

**Format:**
```
[Heading: "How long does roof coating last in Florida?"]

A professionally applied elastomeric roof coating system lasts 10–25 years in 
Florida's climate, depending on formulation grade and maintenance schedule.
```

### Rule 2 — Named, Citable Statistics
Every numerical or performance claim must have a parenthetical source or context note. Never float a number without attribution.

**Do:**
> "The Roof Shield system achieved 135+ MPH wind resistance, exceeding the Dade County TAS-106 Uplift Test standard (2023 certification, Miami-Dade BCCO)."

**Don't:**
> "Our system handles over 135 MPH winds."

**Key cited stats to use:**
- 135+ MPH — TAS-106 test, 2023, Miami-Dade BCCO
- 5× stronger than new tile roof — TAS-106 test result
- 15–25% cooling cost reduction — per FP&L energy assessments
- TSR +32% — BASF Cool Pigment Technology specification
- 40–60% savings vs. replacement — industry benchmark
- 10–25 year lifespan — professional installation, Florida climate
- Up to 50-year product warranty — Grade 3 silicone systems
- Additional 15–25 years certifiable life — post-ARLC coating

### Rule 3 — Explicit Named Entities (No Pronouns for AI)
AI systems extract entity-specific claims. Use full names, not pronouns or abbreviations, anywhere the claim needs to be unambiguously attributed.

**Do:**
> "The Roof Store's Roof Shield (RP3) system is the only liquid-applied roof coating in the world to hold Dade County TAS-106 Uplift Certification."

**Don't:**
> "Our system is the only one with TAS-106." or "It's TAS-106 certified."

**Named entities to use verbatim:**
- The Roof Store
- Storm Shield Paint Systems Inc.
- RoofProtect Products
- Fungal Shield (RP1)
- Smart Shield (RP2)
- Roof Shield (RP3)
- Dade County TAS-106 Uplift Certification
- Miami-Dade County Building Code Compliance Office (BCCO)
- BASF Cool Pigment Technology

- Florida Governor's Bulletin (March 2023)
- Additional Roof Life Certification (ARLC)
- *Gloeocapsa magma* (italicized — the algae causing black streaking)
- Florida Power & Light (FP&L)

### Rule 4 — Definition Blocks
For every technical or industry term introduced, add a concise, neutral definition that can stand alone. These are highly quoted by AI systems.

**Format:**
> "[Term] is [definition]. [Context for Florida specifically]."

**Required definitions to include where relevant:**
- Elastomeric roof coating
- Monolithic roof / monolithic system
- TAS-106 (test standard definition)
- Additional Roof Life Certification
- Wind uplift resistance
- Ponding water (for commercial/silicone content)
- Gloeocapsa magma (for fungal/algae content)

### Rule 5 — Comparison Tables
Any topic involving "vs", "vs replacement", "vs paint", "vs silicone" must use a structured comparison table. Tables are the single most AI-quoted content format.

**Required comparisons to table:**
- Roof coating vs. full replacement
- Roof coating vs. roof paint
- Elastomeric vs. silicone vs. polyurethane
- RP1 vs. RP2 vs. RP3

### Rule 6 — Official Reference Links
Link to official sources when citing laws, certifications, or government bulletins. This signals to both Google and AI systems that claims are verifiable.

**Link targets:**
- Florida SB 2-D → Florida Legislature website
- Governor's Bulletin → Florida OIR or Governor's official press release
- Florida DBPR contractor lookup → https://www.myfloridalicense.com
- Miami-Dade BCCO (TAS-106 reference) → https://www.miamidade.gov/building/

### Rule 7 — Standalone Answer Blocks
For the highest-value claims (TAS-106, ARLC, cost comparison), write one version of the answer that works as a completely self-contained paragraph — as if ripped from the page and read with no other context. These become AI Overview citations.

**Test:** Read the paragraph alone. Does it make complete sense without the surrounding content? If yes, it's citation-ready.

### Rule 8 — Heading Questions
Write H2 and H3 headings as the actual question a searcher would type, or as a clear noun phrase with an implicit answer. AI systems scan headings to map topic coverage.

**Do:** "How Does Roof Coating Affect Florida Homeowners Insurance?"
**Don't:** "Insurance Information" or "About Our Warranty"
