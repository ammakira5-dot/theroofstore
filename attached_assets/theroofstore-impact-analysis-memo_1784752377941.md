## 4. What's Actually Needed to Quantify Real Harm

To convert the estimate into defensible numbers, you need three data sources — all things you likely already have access to right now:

### A. Google Search Console (if connected) — Branded Query Data
- Pull 5 years of impressions/clicks for the query **"the roof store"** and close variants ("roof store florida", "roofstore", etc.)
- Look for anomalies: impression share drops, CTR declines, or click volume plateaus starting around August 2021 that don't correlate with any of your own site changes
- This is the single most direct, defensible metric — it shows Google *literally* splitting branded search intent between two entities

### B. Google Analytics (GA4) — Direct & Referral Traffic Patterns
- Direct traffic trend over 5 years — a legitimate brand with growing offline/word-of-mouth presence should show steady or growing direct traffic; an unexplained flattening or decline is circumstantial evidence of diversion
- Referral traffic from YouTube/Roofing Insights properties (almost certainly zero — because the traffic is going to theroof.store, not you — which is itself the point: **the absence of referral traffic from a channel that talks about "The Roof Store" is evidence of diversion**, not something to search for on your side)
- Any customer-reported "I found you through [X] but ended up somewhere else" notes in CRM/call logs

### C. Third-Party SEO Tool (Ahrefs, SEMrush, or similar) — Side-by-Side Comparison
- Domain Rating / Authority trend for both domains since Aug 2021
- Keyword overlap report — how many of the same queries both domains rank for, and who wins the SERP position for ambiguous "the roof store" queries
- Backlink profile comparison — does theroof.store have backlinks using anchor text that references your business name or location (Florida)?
- This is exactly what your own Section XVI, item 3 already calls for — it hasn't been run yet
