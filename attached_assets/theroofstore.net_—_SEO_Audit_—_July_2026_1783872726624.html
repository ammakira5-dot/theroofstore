1. Executive Summary

B+ Grade

theroofstore.net is a well-built local service site with strong technical fundamentals, rich schema markup, and a clearly differentiated content strategy built around Florida's insurance crisis and the roof coating alternative narrative. The site targets a high-intent, geographically defined audience (South Florida homeowners facing Citizens Insurance pressure or roof replacement quotes) and positions the brand effectively around its core differentiators — TAS-106 certification, 30-year heritage, and in-house manufacturing.

✓ Strengths
  • Canonical, robots.txt, and sitemap are all correctly configured
  • Strong schema coverage: RoofingContractor, FAQPage, Service, BreadcrumbList, Article, ItemList
  • FAQ content is substantive and FAQPage schema is deployed on homepage and article pages
  • Service area pages (100+ cities) well-structured with local schema
  • Blog covers high-intent insurance/certification topics with real authority content
  • Open Graph and Twitter Card tags complete across all pages
  • Geo meta tags (geo.region, geo.position, ICBM) deployed on homepage
⚠ Top Issues
  • Duplicate meta tags — all pages inject OG/meta tags twice (critical rendering artifact)
  • Title tag formula inconsistency — some pages use pipe separators, others use em-dash; brand suffix varies
  • FAQ heading hierarchy — H3s used for FAQ items on homepage where H2 would be more appropriate for FAQ section headings
  • Blog has WordPress fingerprint — blog subdirectory returns "Just another WordPress site" in title/description
  • Legacy .php URLs indexed — old .php pages (broward-county.php, contact.php) still crawlable and indexed
  • Missing Review schema aggregation — only 10 reviews in aggregateRating; no third-party review embeds visible
  • GEO gap — no AI-overview-optimized structured answer blocks beyond FAQ schema

3. Technical Health

Mostly Healthy

✓ robots.txt — Correctly Configured

Global crawl allowed. Only /admin, /trademark-evidence-file, and /trademark-monitoring-log are disallowed. Sitemap declared at https://www.theroofstore.net/sitemap.xml.

✓ Sitemap — Comprehensive

137+ URLs included covering homepage, service pages, product pages, blog, service area hierarchy (county → city level), and editorial articles. All entries show lastmod: 2026-07-11 — indicating either accurate daily crawl or a static date. Confirm lastmod is dynamically updated on content change, not hardcoded.

✓ Canonicalization — Clean

All audited pages declare correct self-referencing canonicals pointing to the https://www.theroofstore.net/ version. No canonical conflicts detected between www and non-www (non-www appears to redirect correctly).

⚠ Critical: Duplicate Meta Tags Across All Pages

All pages inject OG tags, meta description, robots, and Twitter Card tags twice in the HTML <head>. Examples observed: og:title appears as two identical elements; og:description duplicated; robots directive appears as index, follow, index, follow. This indicates a template-level double-injection bug — likely the site's meta tag component is rendered twice (possibly a CMS header partial included twice, or both a base template and page template injecting the same block).

Impact: Google and social crawlers handle this gracefully in most cases, but it creates validation errors in structured data testing, can cause unpredictable OG rendering on social shares, and adds unnecessary page weight. It also makes it harder to audit meta uniqueness cleanly.

Fix: Locate the template component responsible for injecting <meta name="description">, OG tags, and Twitter Card tags, and ensure it is called only once per page render. Check for double inclusion of a header partial or a base layout template conflict.

Impact: HighConfidence: HighEase: MediumPriority: P1

⚠ Legacy .php URLs Still Indexable

Web search results surface legacy pages: /fl/broward-county.php, /contact.php, /factory.php. These are not in the current sitemap, but are indexed by Google. They carry old content ("celebrating our 25th year in 2019"), which may dilute brand authority and confuse searchers who land on them.

Fix: 301-redirect all legacy .php URLs to their equivalent current pages. Add a catch-all redirect rule: /*.php → /[equivalent-slug] or / for pages with no equivalent.

Impact: MediumConfidence: HighEase: MediumPriority: P2

⚠ Blog Subdirectory — WordPress Title Leaking

The blog index at https://theroofstore.net/blog/ shows the title "Just another WordPress site — The Roof Store" in Google's index. This confirms the blog runs on a WordPress install and that the default WordPress tagline was never removed. It presents unprofessionally in SERPs and damages brand perception on blog click-throughs.

Fix: In WordPress → Settings → General, update the Site Title to "The Roof Store" and remove or change the Tagline from "Just another WordPress site" to something meaningful, e.g., "Roof Coating Knowledge Base".

Impact: MediumConfidence: HighEase: EasyPriority: P1 (quick win)

ℹ Sitemap lastmod Static Date — Confirm Dynamic

All 137+ sitemap entries show lastmod: 2026-07-11. If this is a static hardcoded date rather than a dynamic last-modified timestamp, Google will eventually discount it. Verify the sitemap generator updates lastmod based on actual page content change timestamps.

Impact: LowConfidence: MediumEase: EasyPriority: P3

⚠ H1 on /faq is Generic

The FAQ page H1 reads "Frequently Asked Questions" — a missed keyword opportunity. The FAQ page contains rich long-tail content about elastomeric coatings, hurricane protection, and Florida insurance. Suggested H1: "Roof Coating FAQ — Florida Homeowners' Top Questions Answered".

⚠ Homepage H2/H3 Hierarchy — FAQ Section Inconsistency

The "Common Questions" section on the homepage uses H3 for individual FAQ entries. For the FAQPage schema to align well with heading structure (and for Google's rich result parser), FAQ entries under a section should use H3 under an H2 section header — which is correct here — but the H2 heading itself says "Common Questions" rather than a keyword-rich phrase. Suggested H2: "Frequently Asked Questions About Roof Coating in Florida".

Impact: LowConfidence: HighEase: EasyPriority: P3

ℹ Title Separator Inconsistency

Homepage uses | (pipe) while some pages use — (em-dash) as the separator between the page title and brand. Standardize to one separator site-wide for a consistent SERP appearance. Pipe is more common for local service businesses; em-dash works better for editorial/article pages. Consider: pipes for service/product pages, em-dash for articles.

⚠ AggregateRating reviewCount is 10 — Very Low

The homepage RoofingContractor schema declares "ratingValue": "5.0" with only "reviewCount": "10". Google may suppress rich snippet eligibility for star ratings when review counts are very low, or may show the count alongside the stars (showing "5.0 (10)" in SERPs — which can undermine credibility for a 30-year-old business). The site has a /reviews page but no visible mechanism importing Google or third-party review counts into schema dynamically.

Recommendation: Implement a dynamic review sync — import Google Business Profile reviews via API and update aggregateRating count/value server-side. A business with 30 years of operations should have significantly more than 10 documented reviews. Alternatively, link the GBP review count as a data source.

Impact: HighConfidence: HighEase: MediumPriority: P1

ℹ Article Schema Missing datePublished / dateModified

The /roof-replacement-alternative-florida article page uses Article schema but the crawl shows no explicit datePublished or dateModified property in the JSON-LD (only an in-page "Updated July 2026" text label). Add these date fields to the Article schema for Google News eligibility and freshness signals.

Impact: MediumConfidence: HighEase: EasyPriority: P2

ℹ sameAs Array — Only 3 Entries, Missing Key Profiles

The RoofingContractor sameAs array includes the site's own URL and two Google Maps links. Missing: BBB profile URL, Facebook page URL, Yelp page URL. Adding these strengthens entity resolution for Google's Knowledge Graph and improves local pack trust signals.

Impact: MediumConfidence: HighEase: EasyPriority: P2

6. Content & E-E-A-T

Strong Foundation

Content quality is a genuine differentiator. The site avoids thin copy and actively builds real expertise signals — the "30 years," "A+ BBB," "TAS-106 rated," "RoofProtect manufacturer" claims give strong E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) signals for a YMYL-adjacent local service.

✓ Blog — High-Authority Content Strategy

4 blog posts (all September/August 2024) covering Citizens Insurance policy changes, Senate Bill 2-D, and the Additional Roof Life Certification. These target informational queries with genuine legislative sourcing and practical homeowner guidance. This is exactly the kind of content Google's Helpful Content system rewards in the local service space.

✓ FAQ Content — Substantive and Keyword-Rich

Both the homepage FAQs and the standalone /faq page contain detailed, specific answers. The /faq page has expanded to cover 19 questions including pressure cleaning, soft washing, silicone vs. elastomeric — covering long-tail queries well. FAQPage schema is deployed, enabling rich results.

⚠ Blog Content Freshness — All Posts from August–September 2024

All 4 blog posts are dated September 2024 (or August 2024). No posts since then — nearly 10 months gap as of this audit. For a service site in a market with active regulatory changes (Florida insurance law, Citizens policy updates), content freshness matters for both ranking and authority.

Opportunities for new posts: 2025 Citizens Insurance policy updates, hurricane season 2025 preparation guide, "What the new Florida roof law means for homeowners in 2026", silicone vs. elastomeric deep-dive, before/after project spotlights.

Impact: HighConfidence: HighEase: MediumPriority: P1

⚠ No Dedicated "Tile Roof Painting" Landing Page

Queries like "tile roof painting fort lauderdale", "roof painting south florida", and "roof paint vs coating" are commercially high-intent. The site mentions tile roof painting within service area pages (e.g., Fort Lauderdale) and the /faq, but there is no standalone page targeting the "roof painting" intent — which captures a different audience (homeowners thinking of paint, not coating). A page at /tile-roof-painting or /roof-painting-florida could intercept this audience before the comparison is made.

Impact: HighConfidence: HighEase: MediumPriority: P1

ℹ "Silicone Roof Coating" Page — Not in Sitemap

Multiple pages link to /silicone-roof-coating (e.g., the /roof-systems page links: "Learn more about silicone roof coating →"), but this URL does not appear in the sitemap and may not exist yet — or may be a separate page that wasn't included. Verify the page exists and add to sitemap if so.

Impact: MediumConfidence: MediumEase: EasyPriority: P2

ℹ /questions Page — Duplicate FAQ Risk

The sitemap includes both /faq and /questions — both marked changefreq: yearly, priority: 0.7. If these two pages cover similar Q&A content, they risk cannibalizing each other. Audit for content overlap; if duplicate, consolidate into /faq with a 301 from /questions.

Impact: MediumConfidence: MediumEase: EasyPriority: P2

7. Local SEO & Service Area Pages

Well-Built

The service area architecture is one of the most comprehensive on a local roofing site. 100+ city pages across 4 counties (Broward, Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, Monroe) with individual schema, BreadcrumbList, and city-specific body copy.

✓ City Page Structure — Strong Local Signals

Fort Lauderdale (representative sample): Unique H1, city-specific body copy referencing local geography (Intracoastal, salt air), embedded contact form, per-city RoofingContractor schema with GeoCoordinates, BreadcrumbList, and Service schema. This is well above average for local service page SEO.

⚠ Dual Service Area + Roof Services URL Pattern — Potential Thin Content Risk

The sitemap shows parallel URL structures for each county: both /service-areas/broward-county and /roof-services/broward-county — and similarly for Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, and Monroe. These two URL trees (service-areas/* and roof-services/*) may contain near-duplicate content targeting the same geographic + service keywords. If the content is substantially similar, Google may treat one set as thin or choose which to rank, diluting authority.

Recommendation: Audit the content difference between /service-areas/[county] and /roof-services/[county]. If the pages serve different intents (geographic coverage vs. service-specific), differentiate them more clearly. If they are near-duplicates, consolidate using canonical tags pointing to the preferred version.

Impact: HighConfidence: MediumEase: MediumPriority: P1

⚠ og:image Uses Relative Path on City Pages

The Fort Lauderdale page og:image value is /api/storage/public-objects/cities/1484199383121-dfa3c30608cd.jpg — a relative path. OG image tags must be absolute URLs for social sharing to work correctly. Facebook, Twitter/X, and LinkedIn will fail to load the preview image if the URL is not absolute. Fix: prepend https://www.theroofstore.net to all OG image values on city pages.

Impact: MediumConfidence: HighEase: EasyPriority: P2

ℹ Monroe County / Key West — Thin Coverage

Monroe County appears in the sitemap with only the county-level and Key West city pages. Broward has 28+ cities; Miami-Dade has 30+; Palm Beach has 37+. Monroe has 2. If Monroe County is genuinely served, expand city coverage. If not, consider removing those pages or noindexing until content is ready.

Impact: LowConfidence: MediumEase: EasyPriority: P3

8. GEO / AI Search Visibility

Opportunity

The site has a strong foundation for AI search visibility but has not yet maximized it. FAQPage schema with detailed answers is the most effective GEO signal currently deployed.

✓ FAQ Schema — AI Overview Eligible

FAQPage schema with full question/answer pairs is deployed on the homepage and the /roof-replacement-alternative-florida article page. Google AI Overviews commonly pull from FAQPage-structured content for local service queries. The answers are factual, specific, and locally relevant — ideal for AI citation.

⚠ No HowTo Schema — Missed AI Snippet Opportunity

Queries like "how to waterproof a tile roof in Florida" and "how to seal a flat roof after pressure cleaning" are answered in the FAQ but without HowTo schema. Google's AI Overviews and Bing Copilot frequently surface HowTo-structured content for procedural queries. Add HowTo schema to the FAQ answers covering multi-step processes.

Impact: MediumConfidence: HighEase: MediumPriority: P2

⚠ No Explicit "Why Choose" / Comparison Structured Content

AI models generating answers to "best roof coating contractor south florida" or "roof coating vs replacement florida" favor pages with clear comparison structure — tables, spec lists, named certifications. The /roof-replacement-alternative-florida page has a good comparison table, but it lacks schema marking it as a comparative analysis (e.g., ItemList of comparison points, or structured Table with proper header semantics recognized by AI crawlers). This is a low-cost optimization for a page that targets a high-intent query.

Impact: MediumConfidence: MediumEase: MediumPriority: P2

ℹ Entity Consolidation — "The Roof Store" vs. "Storm Shield Paint Systems Inc."

The site uses two business names: "The Roof Store" (brand/DBA) and "Storm Shield Paint Systems Inc." (legal entity, appears in copyright and author tags). Google's Knowledge Graph may treat these as two separate entities. For consistent entity resolution, ensure the Google Business Profile, BBB profile, and all schema use a single consistent primary name ("The Roof Store") with "Storm Shield Paint Systems Inc." noted as the legal name (legalName property in schema), not as the primary name.

Impact: MediumConfidence: MediumEase: EasyPriority: P2
